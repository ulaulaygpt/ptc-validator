ACS-6 — conceptual background

ACS-6 was an earlier conceptual stage in the development of verifiable transparency for human–AI co-creation. The current operational standard is the Canal Transparency Protocol (PTC) and its specification corpus.

Its place today

Current standard

PTC

The PTC corpus defines the current technical rules implemented by Validator. It is the reference for creating, verifying and implementing the protocol.

Predecessor

ACS-6

ACS-6 is preserved as a record of an earlier conceptual stage. It does not replace or amend the current specifications.

What ACS-6 contributed

  • The idea of replacing suspicion or detection with an explicit, verifiable declaration.
  • A way to describe human–AI collaboration without turning transparency into surveillance.
  • The conceptual starting point from which PTC later evolved.
Reading ACS-6 is not required to use Validator or implement PTC. If they differ, the current PTC corpus prevails.
